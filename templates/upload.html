About nail melanoma

A rare skin cancer that grows under the nail. Spotted early, it is treatable.

What it looks like

Most often a brown or black stripe running from the cuticle to the tip of one nail. The stripe may widen, darken or blur at the edges over weeks or months.

Some cases are pink or have no colour at all, so any nail change that does not heal deserves a check.

Warning signs (ABCDEF)
AAge: most common between 50 and 70
BBand: brown or black, wider than 3 mm, blurry edge
CChange: band grows or darkens
DDigit: usually thumb, big toe or index finger
EExtension: colour spreads onto the skin beside the nail
FFamily or personal history of melanoma
Often mistaken for

Bruises under the nail, fungal infection and harmless pigment bands are far more common than melanoma. A bruise grows out with the nail; melanoma does not.

See a doctor if

A dark stripe appears without an injury, keeps widening, or the skin beside the nail turns dark. Only a dermatologist can confirm it, usually with a small biopsy.
